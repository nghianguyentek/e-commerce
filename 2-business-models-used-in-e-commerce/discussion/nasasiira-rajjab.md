Online businesses create value differently from traditional businesses because they rely on digital platforms, customer
data, and continuous interaction rather than only selling physical products. Amazon is a good example because it
operates as a platform that connects millions of buyers, sellers, logistics providers, and service partners within one
ecosystem instead of functioning like a conventional retailer. This allows Amazon to offer a wide variety of products,
personalized recommendations, fast delivery, and convenient payment options while continuously improving customer
experience through data analytics and artificial intelligence (Ronteau et al., 2023). Unlike many traditional
businesses, Amazon also captures value from marketplace commissions, subscriptions such as Prime, cloud services, and
advertising.

The success of e-commerce business models largely depends on how well they build customer trust, remove transaction
barriers, and adapt to changing consumer needs. Businesses that use data to understand customer behavior and provide
seamless experiences across websites, mobile apps, and social media are more likely to grow and retain customers (
Solomons et al., 2022). In Uganda, Jumia Uganda has remained relevant because it combines online shopping with delivery
services and digital payment options, making purchasing easier for customers.

However, not all e-commerce businesses succeed. A model can fail when it lacks trust, reliable logistics, or a clear
value proposition. For example, many fraudulent online stores promoted through Facebook or WhatsApp disappear after
receiving payments because customers have no protection or reliable way to verify sellers. Such businesses lose
credibility quickly and fail to build long-term relationships. In contrast, Amazon thrives because it has established
strong reputation systems, secure payment methods, customer reviews, and efficient delivery networks that encourage
repeat purchases and customer loyalty (Ronteau et al., 2023).

Overall, successful e-commerce businesses compete by creating digital ecosystems that prioritize convenience, trust,
personalization, and long-term customer relationships rather than focusing only on individual sales.
