# UNIT 2 - Digital Value Creation: Why E-Commerce Models Thrive or Fail

by Pascale Liautaud at 06:38 September 13, 2026

Technology has transformed how businesses create, deliver, and capture value. Digital business models allow companies to
interact directly with customers and use data to improve the value they provide. However, technology alone does not
guarantee success; businesses must create value that attracts and retains customers while developing a sustainable
business model.

Ronteau et al. (2023) define value creation as “the production of value or benefit for its consumers by the company” (p.
17). Traditional businesses generally follow a more linear process of delivering value to customers, whereas digital
businesses enable two-way interactions in which customer data and feedback can influence the value created. Solomons et
al. (2022) emphasize data-driven decision-making to better understand customers. HubSpot illustrates this through its
subscription-based model, integrating marketing, CRM, sales, and customer-service functions on one platform. This
creates complementarities, one of Ronteau et al.’s (2023) digital value drivers, because combining services increases
value while helping businesses respond to customer needs.

To thrive, e-commerce models must provide a strong value proposition, understand customer needs, scale operations, and
retain customers through effective digital value drivers. Ronteau et al. (2023), drawing on Amit and Zott, identify four
drivers: efficiency, complementarities, lock-in, and novelty. Amazon demonstrates these through efficient product
search, purchasing, and delivery, complementary services such as Prime, and an ecosystem that encourages repeat
purchases and retention.

However, Groupon illustrates how an e-commerce model can struggle when it fails to create lasting value for both sides
of its platform. Groupon attracted customers with discounted deals, but these customers were not necessarily locked into
individual merchants or the platform. As merchants left, fewer deals were available, making Groupon less attractive to
customers and further reducing its value to merchants. This cycle is known as a reverse network effect (ilny, 2015).

Successful e-commerce models must therefore continually create value for all participants while adapting to changing
customer needs.
