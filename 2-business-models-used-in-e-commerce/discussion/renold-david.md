## Introduction

E-commerce has helped companies (especially those with a narrow reach, like small, local businesses) gain access to a
wider market by providing cheaper and more efficient sales and distribution channels for their products or services.

## Question 1.

A successful commerce strategy then is a holistic endeavor across an organization, focused on personalization and
fostering customer loyalty even in deeply uncertain times. Berkman et al. (n.d.).

Amazon is the world's largest online retailer by market cap. The company began as an online bookseller and has since
grown to encompass virtually every retail category. Reiff, N. (2026). The company launched its business with an

## Question 2.

Many eCommerce businesses struggle after launch because they focus on building the store but ignore what happens next.
Successful eCommerce brands understand that growth requires continuous improvement, optimization, and customer-focused
strategies. Mallick, H. (2026).

### Why eCommerce Businesses Fail

#### Poor Product-Market Fit

Many eCommerce startups fail because they haven't properly validated their product-market fit. Tamakuwala, Y. (2024).

Example: Juicero, a startup that raised $120 million to sell a $400 juicer, failed spectacularly when it was revealed
that their proprietary juice packs could be squeezed by hand just as effectively.

#### Poor Website User Experience

A confusing, slow, or unattractive website can drive potential customers away. Tamakuwala, Y. (2024).

Example: In contrast to failures, Amazon's success can be partly attributed to its continuous focus on improving user
experience, from one-click ordering to personalized recommendations.

#### Lack of Marketing Strategy

Many eCommerce businesses underestimate the importance of a comprehensive marketing strategy. Without effective
marketing, even the best products can go unnoticed. Tamakuwala, Y. (2024).

Example: Dollar Shave Club disrupted the razor industry not just with their product, but with their viral marketing
videos and subscription model.

#### Insufficient Capital

Underestimating the costs of running an eCommerce business can lead to cash flow problems. Tamakuwala, Y. (2024).

Example: Fab.com
raised $336 million but burned through it quickly due to rapid expansion and high operating costs, eventually selling for just $
15 million.

#### Poor Inventory Management

Inefficient inventory management can lead to stockouts or overstock situations, both of which can be costly for an
eCommerce business. Tamakuwala, Y. (2024).

Example: Zappos, now owned by Amazon, built its success partly on its efficient inventory management system, which
allows it to offer a wide range of products with quick delivery times.

#### Neglecting Customer Service

In the competitive eCommerce landscape, poor customer service can quickly lead to lost sales and a damaged reputation.
Tamakuwala, Y. (2024).

Example: Zappos, again, stands out for its exceptional customer service, which has become a key differentiator in the
crowded online shoe market.

## Conclusion

Successful e-commerce companies create value by using technology to provide customers with convenience, accessibility,
variety, and personalized experiences. Amazon demonstrates how a company can combine a strong digital platform with
multiple revenue sources to remain competitive. The success of an e-commerce business depends on understanding customer
needs, using technology effectively, managing costs, and developing a business model that can remain profitable over the
long term.
