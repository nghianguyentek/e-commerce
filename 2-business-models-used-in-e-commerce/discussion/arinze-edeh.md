## E-Commerce Business Models and Digital Value Creation

Successful e-commerce companies create and deliver value differently from traditional businesses because digital
technologies allow them to connect customers, suppliers, data, and services at much greater scale. Amazon is a strong
example. Unlike a traditional retailer that primarily purchases inventory and resells it to customers, Amazon combines
retailing with a multi-sided platform that connects buyers and third-party sellers. This model expands product variety
while allowing Amazon to scale without owning all the inventory. It also uses customer data, personalization, reviews,
and digital infrastructure to improve convenience and reduce search and transaction costs (Ronteau et al., 2023). These
capabilities reflect important digital value drivers such as efficiency, complementarities, lock-in, and novelty.

E-commerce models can fail when they do not create sufficient customer value, cannot achieve sustainable revenue, or
fail to attract enough participants to the platform. For example, the failure of Quibi demonstrates the importance of
matching a digital business model with customer needs. Quibi launched a subscription-based mobile video service but
struggled to attract and retain users despite substantial investment. Its model faced strong competition and did not
provide enough differentiated value to overcome established alternatives. By contrast, successful platforms benefit from
network effects, where increasing participation can make the service more valuable to other users (Hagiu & Wright,
2015).

Therefore, e-commerce success depends not simply on having an online presence but on designing a business model that
creates distinctive value, delivers it efficiently, and captures sufficient economic value to remain sustainable.
