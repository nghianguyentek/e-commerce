Successful e-commerce companies create and deliver value differently from traditional businesses because they use technology to connect customers, sellers, and other services in one digital ecosystem. Amazon is a good example. A traditional retailer normally buys products, keeps inventory, and sells them directly to customers. Amazon also sells products, but it operates a marketplace where third-party sellers can reach millions of customers. This allows Amazon to offer a large variety of products without owning all the inventory. Digital business models can create value through efficiency, complementarities, lock-in, and novelty (Bican & Brem, 2022a). For example, Amazon creates efficiency by making searching, purchasing, and payment easier for customers.

Amazon also benefits from network effects. When more sellers join the platform, customers have more products to choose from. At the same time, more customers make the platform more attractive to sellers (Bican & Brem, 2022b). This is different from the more linear relationship found in many traditional businesses.

However, not every e-commerce business model succeeds. One reason for failure is having many customers without a sustainable way to make a profit. MoviePass is a good example. Its subscription was attractive, but frequent customer usage created costs that its low monthly price could not support (Bican & Brem, 2022c). In contrast, Amazon continues to create value through its marketplace, technology, logistics, data, and complementary services. I think a digital company needs more than a good idea or many users. It must create customer value while also finding a sustainable way to capture value.
