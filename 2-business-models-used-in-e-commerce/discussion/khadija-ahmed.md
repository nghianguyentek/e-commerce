## Digital Business Models and E-Commerce Success

Successful e-commerce companies create and deliver value differently from traditional businesses by using digital technologies to increase efficiency, customer engagement, scalability, and personalization. Amazon is a strong example because it operates not only as an online retailer but also as a digital platform connecting customers, sellers, and complementary services. Ronteau et al. (2023) identify efficiency, complementarities, lock-in, and novelty as important drivers of digital value creation. Amazon applies these drivers by providing a broad product ecosystem, personalized recommendations, convenient services, and an integrated marketplace. Its platform also benefits from network effects, where greater participation from buyers and sellers can increase the platform’s overall value.

Data analytics further strengthens this model. By collecting and analyzing customer data, digital businesses can better understand customer preferences and improve targeting, personalization, and customer relationships (Solomons et al., 2022). This creates value more dynamically than many traditional businesses that rely primarily on physical locations and face-to-face transactions.

However, not every e-commerce model succeeds. Webvan provides an example of failure because it invested heavily in dedicated distribution infrastructure and attempted rapid expansion before establishing a sustainable business model. Stanford’s case study notes that Webvan developed an extensive infrastructure for nationwide online grocery delivery, but its business model faced significant challenges as the company expanded (Mendelson, 2001). This demonstrates that technological sophistication alone does not guarantee success. Ultimately, e-commerce businesses thrive when technology, customer value, scalability, and sustainable value capture are aligned.
