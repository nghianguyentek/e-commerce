Successful e-commerce companies create and deliver value differently from traditional businesses by using digital technologies to reduce transaction costs, increase convenience, personalize experiences, and operate at a much larger scale. Amazon, for example, combines an online marketplace with logistics, customer data, recommendations, and complementary services. This creates value not only by selling products but also by connecting customers with numerous sellers and making the purchasing process more convenient. Ronteau et al. (2023) identify efficiency, complementarities, lock-in, and novelty as important drivers of value in digital business models. Amazon demonstrates several of these drivers by combining products and services while using digital information to improve the customer experience.

Successful e-commerce models also tend to use customer data to understand behavior and continuously improve their offerings. Data-driven decision-making allows businesses to evaluate customer interactions and develop more relevant marketing and experiences (Solomons et al., 2022).

In contrast, e-commerce models can fail when businesses prioritize rapid expansion and technology over customer needs, financial sustainability, and market readiness. Boo.com provides a useful example. The company attempted an ambitious international launch and invested heavily in sophisticated website technology, but its website was slow and difficult for many customers to use. The company also expanded faster than its sales could support, contributing to its eventual failure (Toufaily & Pons, 2001). This demonstrates that digital innovation alone does not guarantee success. E-commerce businesses must combine technology with a sustainable business model, effective customer value creation, and realistic financial management.
