Traditional brick-and-mortar stores rely on linear supply chains and physical shelf space, which inherently restricts
their inventory variety and geographical reach. In contrast, modern digital platforms like Amazon create and deliver
value by functioning as multi-sided ecosystems that connect millions of independent buyers and sellers across the
globe (Ronteau et al., 2023). Instead of depending entirely on owning physical merchandise, Amazon leverages digital
infrastructure, network effects, and customer analytics to personalize shopping, streamline logistics, and minimize
transaction costs for everyone involved (Solomons et al., 2022). This flexible foundation allows the platform to scale
smoothly, capture value through varied streams such as marketplace seller fees and digital services, and ensure rapid
delivery without incurring the immense real estate costs required by traditional retail operations.

The difference between digital success and failure frequently comes down to unit economics, logistical scalability, and
customer retention (Ronteau et al., 2023). Thriving e-commerce businesses succeed because they eliminate buyer friction,
generate continuous trust, and harness positive network effects that make the platform increasingly useful as more
people join. For instance, Amazon thrives because services like Prime establish strong customer loyalty, which locks
shoppers into recurring purchases while driving down marketing acquisition costs. Conversely, failed e-commerce ventures
often expand rapidly without fixing high fulfillment expenses or validating sustainable unit margins. A notable example
is Pets.com, which collapsed during the early internet boom because shipping bulky, heavy pet food created massive
operational losses that far exceeded its profit margins (Solomons et al., 2022). Without resolving logistical hurdles or
building viable repeat economics, online models quickly exhaust their capital and fail.
