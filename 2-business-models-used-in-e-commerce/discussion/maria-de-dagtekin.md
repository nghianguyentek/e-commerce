In this discussion, I will analyze digital business models and explain how value is created, delivered, and captured in
online environments. I will also compare and contrast successful and failed e-commerce models, exploring how successful
companies create and deliver value differently, and understand the key reasons why certain e-commerce business models
fail while others thrive in the digital economy.

Successful e-commerce companies like Amazon are able to create and deliver value in a different way that traditional
businesses do by providing convenience and accessibility, a vast product selection and by possessing the logistics
infrastructure to allow for quick fulfillment (Amazon Supply Chain Services, 2026). As an avid Amazon user myself, I
find the amazon app easier to navigate than other retailers, especially when navigating the return or refund process.
Because Amazon has such a vast product selection and two day shipping, most of my online shopping is done on Amazon
without even considering other e-commerce companies.

Some key reasons why Amazons business model is able to thrive in the digital economy are because of the reasons that I
mentioned prior, otherwise described as a strong value proposition for the consumer, Amazons multiple sources of revenue
like Amazon Web Services (AWS) and their continuous adaptation to technology (Schwartz, 2023). An example of a failed
e-commerce business is pets.com, the first online store to shut down during the “dot com” crash (L.A. Times, 2000).
Pets.com failed in large part to a combination to high costs in shipping heavy products like pet food, low consumer
trust in purchasing pet food online at the time, preferring to shop in stores.
