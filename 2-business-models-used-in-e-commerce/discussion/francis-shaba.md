## 1. How Amazon Creates and Delivers Value Differently

Amazon creates and delivers value differently from traditional businesses because it operates as a platform rather than just a seller. Ronteau et al. (2023, Section 3.1) explain that platform businesses like Amazon create multi-sided markets that connect many buyers and sellers at the same time, instead of selling products through a single shop.

According to Ronteau et al. (2023, Section 2.3), digital value comes from four main drivers: efficiency, complementarities, lock-in, and novelty. Amazon uses efficiency through fast delivery and automation. Its marketplace and cloud services create complementarities by offering products and services that work well together. Solomons et al. (2022, Chapter 8) add that using data to personalise services and providing an omnichannel approach make the customer experience smoother and more relevant.

Amazon also captures value through subscriptions, advertising, and commissions. This means it earns money from ongoing services, or “Everything-as-a-Service,” rather than depending only on one-time sales (Ronteau et al., 2023, Section 8.2).

## 2. Why Some E-Commerce Models Fail, and Others Thrive

One important reason some e-commerce models fail is a lack of trust and poor management of the marketplace ecosystem. Ronteau et al. (2023, Section 6.5) describe the “lemons problem,” which happens when buyers cannot easily judge the quality of products. Section 4.2 also explains that operational and ethical challenges can harm digital platforms.

For example, many small online marketplaces failed because they could not build trust or manage logistics effectively. In contrast, successful businesses grow through network effects and the effective use of data. Amazon succeeds because its platform becomes more valuable as more buyers and sellers join. Its use of analytics also helps improve customer retention (Ronteau et al., 2023, Sections 3.3 and 8.3).

Similarly, Airbnb succeeded by combining trust mechanisms with technology to support peer-to-peer ecosystems (Ronteau et al., 2023, Section 6.3; Solomons et al., 2022, Chapter 15).
