Amazon differs from traditional stores in how it creates and delivers value to customers. A traditional store depends on
a location and limited space, so the number of products it can display is limited. In contrast, Amazon allows customers
to shop anywhere and at any time, while providing products that can be easily searched and compared. Sellers can use
Amazon’s platform to reach more customers. Therefore, the value of the platform is not limited to buyers but also
includes sellers.

Amazon uses data to understand what customers search for and purchase, and then uses this information to improve
inventory management and delivery. The company has developed digital services, such as Amazon Marketplace and AWS, which
have helped it expand its business model instead of relying only on product sales (Ronteau et al., 2023).

One reason for Amazon’s success is its ability to adapt to changes, invest in technology, and provide convenience to
customers. Therefore, Amazon can be considered an example of an e-commerce business model that has succeeded in the
digital economy.

In contrast, Toys “R” Us is an example of a business model that failed to adapt. The company was slow to build a strong
online presence and relied on Amazon instead of developing its own digital strategy. As customers increasingly turned to
online shopping, the company became less competitive in terms of convenience and online shopping experience. It also
failed to provide a different experience that encouraged customers to choose it, which contributed to its decline and
liquidation (Basiouny, 2018).

In conclusion, e-commerce business models are more likely to succeed when they focus on technology, customer needs, and
continuous adaptation. They may fail when they continue to rely on traditional approaches and do not respond to changes
in the market.
