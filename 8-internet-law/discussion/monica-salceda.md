Legal frameworks help create trust in e-commerce by establishing rules for how businesses interact with consumers and use their information. One important example is the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). It requires organizations covered by the regulation to process personal data lawfully, fairly, and transparently and to explain purposes for collecting it (European Commission, n.d.). I consider transparency especially important because customers should understand what information a company collects and how it will be used.

Intellectual property protections also encourage fair competition. Copyright protects original creative expression, while trademarks protect identifiers that distinguish the source of goods or services (LawShelf, n.d.). This matters because businesses invest time and money developing photographs, designs, content, and recognizable brands. A recent example involved online retailers Nelly and Shein. In 2025, a Swedish court found that a Shein subsidiary infringed Nelly’s rights by copying photographs and using them on Shein’s Swedish website without permission (Reuters, 2025). This demonstrates why I believe competitors should not be able to benefit from creative work that another business invested in producing.

Finally, AI and data-driven algorithms create ethical concerns surrounding privacy and unequal treatment. Personalization can be useful because it helps customers discover relevant products. However, I become concerned when customers do not know how their data influences what they see or when algorithms treat customers differently, particularly through personalized offers or prices. Ronteau et al. (2023) highlight ethical concerns associated with data-driven business models. Businesses should therefore explain meaningful uses of customer data, avoid unfair discrimination, and provide appropriate human oversight for consequential automated decisions.
