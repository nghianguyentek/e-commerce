## Question 1

Legal frameworks and regulatory organisations play a crucial role in creating safe and trustworthy online business environments. One significant regulation is the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which was implemented in the European Union in 2018. The GDPR establishes strict guidelines for the collection and processing of personal data, ensuring that consumers have control over their information. It mandates that businesses obtain explicit consent before collecting data and provides individuals with the right to access and delete their personal data. By enforcing these regulations, the GDPR protects consumers from data breaches and misuse, fostering trust in online transactions (Ronteau et al., 2023).

## Question 2

Intellectual property protections, such as copyrights and trademarks, are vital for promoting ethical and fair online business practices. For instance, the case of Oracle America, Inc. v. Google, Inc. illustrates the importance of copyright in digital content protection. Oracle sued Google for using its Java programming language in the Android operating system without permission. The court's ruling emphasised the need to respect copyright laws, which helps ensure that creators are compensated for their work, thereby encouraging innovation and fair competition in e-commerce (Solomons et al., 2022).

## Question 3

One ethical challenge that arises when businesses use technologies like AI is the potential for biased algorithms that influence consumer behaviour unfairly. For example, AI systems may inadvertently perpetuate existing biases, leading to discriminatory practices in advertising. To address this challenge, businesses should implement regular audits of their algorithms to ensure fairness and transparency. Additionally, they should engage diverse teams in the development process to minimise bias and promote ethical standards in technology usage (Ronteau et al., 2023).
