**Business and Marketing's Digital Transformation**

The way businesses engage with their customers has been completely transformed by the Internet and new technology, going from one-way transactions to ongoing, data-driven partnerships. Digital platforms enable businesses to rapidly contact consumers throughout the world, tailor their services, and get feedback in real time (Ronteau et al., 2023). Recommendation engines and chatbots that anticipate client demands and offer round-the-clock assistance are now powered by AI, and mobile apps enable convenience-driven services like mobile banking and on-demand delivery. Instead of depending only on advertising, social media has made consumers active players who exchange reviews, interact directly with brands, and influence purchasing decisions through peer networks. These changes have led to the development of new mechanisms for value generation and capture, where companies now make money from data, platforms, and network effects in addition to products (Ronteau et al., 2023).

In line with this, marketing has changed. Conventional marketing promoted generic themes to large audiences using interruptive, one-way media including print, television, and cold calling (Solomons et al., 2022). Instead, inbound and digital marketing concentrate on drawing clients naturally through social media interaction, email nurturing, search engine optimization, and useful content. This strategy fosters trust prior to a sale and enables ongoing relationship management through tailored retargeting and loyalty initiatives. Digital marketing's measurability through analytics tools provides marketers with accurate information into consumer behavior, allowing for ongoing campaign optimization, something that conventional media could rarely provide (Solomons et al., 2022). In general, the customer relationship has become more decentralized, interactive, personalized, and data-driven due to digital transformation.
