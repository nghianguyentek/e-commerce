## Discussion Forum Unit 1

The Internet has changed business from mainly being a physical activity to something that can happen continuously through digital platforms. Customers can now search for products, compare alternatives, communicate with businesses, make purchases, and share their experiences using smartphones. Ronteau et al. (2023) explain that smartphones have become an extension of consumers because they influence how people communicate, obtain information, access services, and purchase products. This has also allowed businesses to use customer data to understand behaviour and provide more personalised experiences. For example, a Ugandan customer can discover a product through social media, contact the seller through WhatsApp, pay using mobile money, and arrange delivery without visiting a physical shop. AI can further support this process through recommendations, automated customer support, and analysis of customer behaviour.

Marketing has also moved from simply putting advertisements in front of people to creating reasons for customers to find and engage with a business. Traditional outbound marketing depends heavily on approaches such as paid advertising and direct communication, while inbound marketing attracts potential customers through useful content, organic traffic, referrals, and other forms of engagement. The course reading notes that inbound activities can reduce customer acquisition costs because customers can be attracted through word-of-mouth, organic traffic, viral content, partnerships, and free trials (Ronteau et al., 2023). Social media makes this approach even more interactive because customers can comment, share, and create content themselves. Therefore, digital marketing is not only about selling; it is about building an ongoing relationship that can attract, satisfy, and retain customers.
