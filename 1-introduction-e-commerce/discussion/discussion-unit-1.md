The Internet and emerging digital technologies have fundamentally changed how businesses interact with and serve customers. Traditionally, businesses relied heavily on physical stores, telephone communication, television, and print advertising. Today, websites, mobile applications, social media, artificial intelligence (AI), and digital platforms enable businesses to interact with customers continuously and across geographical boundaries. Digital technologies also allow companies to collect and analyse customer data, enabling more personalised experiences. For example, companies such as Spotify and Netflix use data and digital technologies to provide personalised content and recommendations, demonstrating how digital business models can create value through customer-focused experiences (Ronteau et al., 2023).

Marketing has similarly shifted from traditional approaches toward digital and inbound strategies. Traditional marketing generally communicates messages to broad audiences through channels such as television, radio, newspapers, and direct advertising. In contrast, inbound marketing focuses on attracting customers by providing relevant and useful content that matches their needs and interests. Digital marketing extends this approach through websites, search engines, social media, email, and other online channels. Content marketing is particularly important because it enables businesses to build relationships with audiences by consistently providing valuable information rather than relying only on promotional messages (Solomons et al., 2022).

In conclusion, the major change is that customers now have greater control over the relationship. They can research products, compare alternatives, communicate with businesses, and share experiences instantly. Consequently, businesses must provide a consistent, personalised, trustworthy, and user-friendly digital experience across multiple channels to attract customers and encourage long-term loyalty.
