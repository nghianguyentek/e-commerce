Social media increases visibility by placing a brand where audiences already spend time and by turning customers into active participants (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010). Gymshark is a clear example. It grew through Instagram, a visual social networking platform, and later TikTok and YouTube. Fitness apparel sells through images and short videos, and these platforms suit the goal of reaching community-minded young customers (Solomons et al., 2022). Its tactics included gifting products to fitness influencers, sharing user-generated content, replying to comments, and running the #gymshark66 habit challenge, where followers tagged the brand and posted their progress (Case Study, n.d.). Reported revenue rose from £176 million in 2019 to £320 million in 2021 (Case Study, n.d.).

Small e-commerce businesses can use two low-cost tactics. The first is a branded hashtag that encourages customers to post photos of their purchases. This works because peer content builds trust and costs almost nothing. The second is fast, friendly replies on WhatsApp Business or Instagram messages, since loyalty grows when customers feel valued and served consistently (Hyken, 2016). A free CRM such as HubSpot, together with Instagram Insights, can record each customer's questions, purchases, and complaints, so the owner can respond quickly and spot recurring problems (Solomons et al., 2022).

An important ethical practice is transparency, meaning clearly disclosing paid partnerships and sponsored posts, for example with #ad (Federal Trade Commission, 2019). Honest data practices matter equally, because customers increasingly question how attention and personal data are used (Ronteau et al., 2023). Transparency matters because trust, once lost, is hard to rebuild, and customers who feel respected return and recommend the brand.
